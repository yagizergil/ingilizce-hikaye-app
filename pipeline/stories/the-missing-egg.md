# A Cake for Mother

Mert and his sister Ada wanted to make a cake. It was their mother's birthday.

"We can do this," Ada said. "I have the list."

She read from an old piece of paper. "Sugar, butter, milk, and two eggs."

Mert got the sugar from the shelf. Ada got the butter and milk from the fridge.

"Now the eggs," Ada said. She opened the fridge again.

There was only one egg.

"We need two," Mert said.

Ada picked up the egg and looked at it. "Maybe one egg is fine."

"The list says two," Mert said. "A cake needs two eggs. One egg is not enough for a good cake."

Ada was quiet for a moment. "Where do we get another egg? Mom is at the shop now. She has the money."

Mert looked at the clock. "She comes back at four. We have one hour."

"One hour is not much time," Ada said.

Mert thought for a moment. "The shop on the corner sells eggs."

"But we have no money," Ada said. "It is all in Mom's bag."

They looked at each other. Ada put the sugar back on the shelf slowly.

"Maybe we ask a neighbor," Mert said.

"Which neighbor?"

"Number five. Mrs. Yıldız always has eggs. She keeps chickens in her yard."

Ada's face was happy now. "That is a good idea."

"We need to go now," Mert said. "Before Mom is at the house again."

They put on their shoes and ran to the stairs. Number five was two floors down.

Mert called at the door. They waited. No answer.

He called again, louder this time.

No answer again.

"Maybe she is not home," Ada said in a quiet voice.

"She has to be home," Mert said. "I saw her this morning."

He called one more time. Then they heard footsteps behind the door.

# The Egg from Number Five

The door opened. Mrs. Yıldız was there in her apron.

"Mert! Ada! What is wrong?"

"We need an egg," Mert said quickly. "Just one. For our mother's cake."

Mrs. Yıldız had a big smile. "Is today her birthday?"

"Yes," Ada said. "We want to make her happy today."

"Come to my kitchen," Mrs. Yıldız said. "My chickens gave me five eggs this morning."

She walked to her kitchen. Two small chickens walked around her yard, near some flowers.

"Your chickens are so cute," Ada said.

"They are a lot of fun too," Mrs. Yıldız said, and laughed. "But I love them."

She picked two eggs from a bowl. "Take two, not one. A good cake needs a little more."

"Thank you very much," Mert said.

They ran back to their kitchen, with the eggs in their hands, like gold. In the kitchen, they mixed the sugar, butter, milk, and eggs together.

"The smell is nice already," Mert said.

They put everything into a pan and put it in the oven.

"Now we wait," Ada said.

They cleaned the kitchen fast. Sugar was on the table, on the floor, even on Mert's nose.

At four o'clock, the front door opened. Their mother walked in, tired from the shop.

"Something smells wonderful," she said.

Mert and Ada ran to her. "Happy birthday!" they said together.

Their mother looked at the kitchen. She saw the cake in the oven through the little glass window.

"Did you two make this alone?"

"Almost alone," Ada said. "Mrs. Yıldız gave us two eggs."

Their mother laughed and put her arms around them both. "This is the best birthday gift."

The cake came out warm and gold. They cut three pieces and ate together at the table.

"Mrs. Yıldız is a very good neighbor," Mert said.

"Everything is better with good neighbors," their mother said. Her face was happy.
