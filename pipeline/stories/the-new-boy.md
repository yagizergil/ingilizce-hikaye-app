# The Boy by the Wall

Every day after school, the boys played football in the yard. Onur always played too.

One day, a new boy was by the wall. He watched the game but did not play.

Onur looked at him between kicks. The boy had dark hair and a blue bag.

"Who is that?" Onur asked his friend Berk.

"New boy," Berk said. "He came this week. I do not know his name."

The ball went near the wall. Onur ran to get it. He stopped close to the new boy.

"Hi," Onur said. "Do you want to play?"

"No, thank you," the boy said fast.

"Why not?"

The boy looked at his shoes. "I do not know the rules here."

"It is easy," Onur said. "You just kick the ball into the goal."

"I am not good at football," the boy said in a quiet voice.

Onur picked up the ball. "What is your name?"

"Kerem," the boy said.

"I am Onur. Where are you from?"

"Another city," Kerem said. "We came here one week ago. My father has a new job."

The bell rang. It was time for class. Onur went back to his class, but he thought about Kerem all through the lesson.

At lunch, Onur saw Kerem sitting alone at the end of a table. Everyone else sat in big groups, talking and laughing.

Onur got his food and walked to Kerem's table.

"Can I sit here?" he asked.

Kerem's eyes opened big. "Sure."

They ate in a quiet way for a moment. Then Onur asked, "Do you play any sport?"

"I play basketball," Kerem said. "Not football."

"We do not have basketball here," Onur said. "Just football, every day."

Kerem pushed his food with his fork. "Then I will just watch."

"That is boring," Onur said.

"It is a little boring," Kerem said. His face was almost happy.

Onur had an idea. It was small, but it felt right.

# One More Player

One more day, Onur came to school early. He found Berk and two other boys near the football goal.

"I have an idea," Onur said. "Let us learn one basketball game. From Kerem."

Berk was not happy. "Why? We do not have a basketball hoop."

"We do not need one," Onur said. "There are other basketball games. Games with passing."

After school, Onur walked straight to Kerem by the wall.

"Can you show us a basketball game?" Onur asked. "One with no hoop?"

Kerem's eyes opened big. "You want to play basketball?"

"Just for today," Onur said. "Then maybe we can play football too."

Kerem thought for a minute. Then his face was happy, a real happy face this time.

"There is a game with passing," he said. "We get in a circle. We pass the ball fast. If a player drops it, that player sits on the ground."

"That is fun," Berk said.

They made a circle with five boys. Kerem was between them. He told them the rules again, slow this time.

The ball went fast from hand to hand. Onur dropped it first and sat on the ground, laughing. Then Berk dropped it too.

Kerem did not drop it one time. He was fast and caught everything.

"You are really good," Onur said.

"Basketball is my favorite," Kerem said. His face was very happy.

When the bell rang, the boys did not want to stop.

"Tomorrow we play football," Onur said. "And you can join us. I will show you the rules again, and I will help you."

"I like that plan," Kerem said.

The day was over. Onur walked out with Kerem and Berk. They talked about the game all the way to the gate.

"Same time tomorrow?" Berk asked.

"Same time," Onur said. "Football and basketball, both."

Kerem was happy on his walk to his house. This was the first day the yard felt good and full for him. It felt like a place for him.
