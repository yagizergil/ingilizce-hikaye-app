# Bus Number Nine

Aylin fell asleep on the bus.

She was tired from the long week. Her head hit the window softly, and she did not feel the bus stop and start again, stop and start again. She did not hear the driver call the last stop.

She opened her eyes when the bus stopped for a long time. The engine was off. Outside, the sky was dark orange. It was almost evening.

She looked around. The bus was empty.

"Excuse me," she said. "Where are we?"

The driver turned around. He was an old man with grey hair and a blue jacket. "This is the end of the line," he said. "Where do you want to go?"

"Home. Yenimah street." Aylin stood up fast. "This is bus number nine, right?"

"No. This is bus number nineteen." The driver looked at her. "You got on the wrong bus, young lady."

Aylin felt her stomach drop. "But I got on at the market. Bus nine always stops there."

"Nine and nineteen stop at the same place today. There is work on the road. Nineteen comes first now." He shook his head. "I am sorry. This bus does not go back tonight. I finish here."

Aylin looked out the window. She did not know this town. There were small houses and a closed shop. No people. No lights except one, far away.

"I must call my mother," she said. She put her hand in her bag.

Her phone was not there.

She checked again, and again. Nothing. She remembered now — she left it on the kitchen table that morning. Her mother was going to be angry. Her mother was also going to worry, because Aylin promised to be home by six for her grandfather's birthday dinner.

It was already six.

"Is there a phone here?" she asked the driver. "Anywhere?"

The driver pointed down the road. "The small shop. Mehmet's shop. He has a phone behind his desk. But he closes at half past six."

Aylin looked at her watch. Twenty-five minutes.

"Thank you," she said, and she ran.

# Mehmet's Shop

The road was long and the light was going fast.

Aylin ran past the closed shop, past three houses, past a dog that watched her but did not move. Her shoes were not good for running, but she did not stop.

She saw a small light ahead. A sign said MEHMET — FOOD AND MORE. The door was still open.

Inside, an old man stood behind a wood desk. He was putting cans into a box.

"Please," Aylin said. She was breathing hard. "Can I use your phone? I am lost. I got on the wrong bus."

Mehmet looked at her kindly. "Of course. It is by the window."

Aylin picked up the phone and called her house. It rang and rang. Nobody answered. Her mother and grandfather were probably already at the restaurant, waiting for her.

She tried her mother's phone next. This time, someone answered.

"Mom!"

"Aylin! Where are you? We are all at the restaurant. Your grandfather keeps looking at the door."

"I got on bus nineteen by mistake. I am in a small town. I don't know the name." Aylin looked at Mehmet. "What is this place called?"

"Karatepe," Mehmet said.

"Karatepe," Aylin said into the phone.

Her mother was quiet for a second. "That is forty minutes from here. How did you get all the way to Karatepe?"

"The road work. Bus nine and nineteen stop at the same place now." Aylin's voice shook a little. "I'm sorry, Mom. I am going to miss the dinner."

"Don't worry about the dinner. I worry about you. Stay where you are. Your uncle has a car. He is going to come and get you."

Mehmet listened to all of this. When Aylin put the phone down, he closed his box of cans and looked at her.

"Your uncle is coming from far away," he said. "Sit down. You can wait here. I am going to make tea."

"You don't have to do that."

"I know," Mehmet said. "But your grandfather has a birthday, and you are far from your family tonight. Tea is the least I can do."

Aylin sat down on an old chair by the window. Mehmet brought two cups of tea and a plate of small biscuits. They talked about the town, about his shop, about her grandfather.

An hour later, headlights moved across the window. Her uncle's car stopped outside.

Aylin stood up fast, but then she turned back to Mehmet. "Thank you. For the phone. For the tea. For everything."

"Go," Mehmet said, smiling. "Your grandfather is waiting."

She ran outside and got into the car. Her uncle drove fast through the dark roads, back towards the restaurant, back towards home.

When they arrived, her grandfather stood up from the table. He did not look angry. He looked happy to see her.

"I heard you had an adventure," he said.

"I got on the wrong bus," Aylin said, and she started to laugh. "The whole town was called Karatepe, and I don't even remember getting there."

Her grandfather laughed too. "Sit down," he said. "Tell me everything. We saved you a piece of cake."

Aylin sat next to him. Outside, the night was dark and cold, but the restaurant was warm and full of light. She was finally home.
