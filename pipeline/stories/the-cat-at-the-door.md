# A Cat in the Rain

Ediz walked to his house in the rain. Water ran along the street. His shoes had no dry part left.

Near his door, he heard a small sound. He stopped and listened.

"Meow."

He looked at the ground. A thin grey cat sat by the wall. Its fur had water in it.

"Hello," Ediz said. He got close to the cat carefully.

The cat did not go away. It looked up at him with big green eyes.

"Are you hungry?" Ediz asked.

The cat looked very thin.

Ediz opened the door and went into the house. He came back with a small piece of chicken.

The cat ate it fast. Then it looked at Ediz again.

"Can I keep you?" Ediz said in a quiet voice.

He picked the cat up. It was light, almost too light. He carried it into the house.

His mother was in the kitchen. She turned and saw the cat.

"Ediz! What is that?"

"A cat," Ediz said. "She was alone in the rain."

His mother put her spoon on the table. "We cannot keep a cat. You know the rules here."

"What rules?"

"Our building does not allow pets. Mr. Aksoy told us last year."

Ediz held the cat close. "But she is very thin. She needs help."

His mother looked at the cat. It began to shake a little in Ediz's arms.

"We can make her warm and give her food," his mother said. "But she cannot stay long."

Ediz got an old towel. He used it on the cat carefully. The cat began to purr.

"Listen to that," Ediz said. "She likes it here."

"Ediz," his mother said, "do not love her too much. We need to find her a new home."

"She already has a home," Ediz said. "Here."

His mother said no with her head. "Not this time."

That night, the cat slept in a box by Ediz's bed. Ediz did not sleep much. He kept thinking about Mr. Aksoy, the rules, and the thin grey cat.

# A Home for Six

In the morning, Ediz had a plan.

He told his friend Kaan at school. "I have a cat. My mother says she cannot stay."

"Why not?" Kaan asked.

"No pets in our building."

Kaan thought for a second. "My aunt lives on Elm Street. She loves cats. She already has two."

"Two more will not hurt then," Ediz said. His face was happy.

After school, Ediz ran to his house. He found the cat asleep in the box. She opened one eye and looked at him.

"I found you a home," he said. "A good one."

His mother came into the room. "Did you find something?"

"Kaan's aunt," Ediz said. "She has two cats already. She wants more."

His mother's face was happy too. "That is good news."

But Ediz did not feel good about it. He liked the cat. He did not want to give her away.

"Can we visit her?" he asked. "After she is at her new home?"

"Of course," his mother said. "Elm Street is close."

That afternoon, they walked to Elm Street with the cat in a small box. Kaan's aunt opened the door. Her name was Sevgi.

"Oh, look at her," Sevgi said. "Very thin! Come in, come in."

Two big cats sat by the window. They looked at the new cat, and they were quiet.

Sevgi took the grey cat in her arms. "Her name is Duman," she said. "Because of her grey color."

Ediz watched Duman sit on a big chair between the other two cats. She looked happy already.

"She likes it here," Ediz said.

"Cats always know a good home," Sevgi said. "Thank you for bringing her to me."

Ediz's mother had a big smile for him. "You did something kind today."

Walking to his house, Ediz felt better. He missed Duman a little, but he knew she was safe and warm.

"Can we visit her again soon?" he asked.

"Every week, if you want," his mother said.

Ediz's face was happy. Some cats find one home. This cat found two.
