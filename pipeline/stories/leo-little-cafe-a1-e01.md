# A Small Café

Leo has a small café. It is across the street from the big train station.

The café is very small. It has five tables and one window. Leo makes the coffee. Leo makes the tea. Leo washes the cups too.

Every morning, Leo opens the door at seven o'clock. He puts music on. He says hello to the street.

At 7:15, Ada comes in. Ada is seventy years old. She is a piano teacher, but she does not work now.

Ada sits at the table by the window. Every day, the same chair. Every day, the same drink: a small black coffee and one orange cake.

"Good morning, Ada," Leo says.

"Good morning, Leo," Ada says. "Is it cold today?"

"Very cold," Leo says. "But the coffee is hot."

Ada likes the window chair. She can see the station. She can see the trains and the people. She can see the birds on the roof.

# The Wrong Chair

On Monday, Leo opens the door at seven o'clock. It is raining.

At 7:05, a young man comes in. He has a big blue bag and a map. He is not from this city.

"Hello," the man says. "A big coffee, please."

He sits at the table by the window. He sits in Ada's chair.

Leo looks at the clock. It is 7:10.

"Oh no," Leo says.

The man opens his map. He is happy. He drinks his coffee. He looks at the trains.

At 7:15, the door opens. Ada comes in. She takes off her wet hat. She looks at the window.

She stops. She looks at the man in her chair. Then she looks at Leo.

Leo does not know what to say. He does not want to make the young man sad. He does not want to make Ada sad.

# One More Chair

Leo has an idea.

He goes into the small room at the back. He finds an old chair. It is red and very small. He carries it to the window.

He puts the red chair next to the man's table.

"Hello," Leo says to the young man. "This is Ada. Every morning she sits here. She knows this city very well. Can she sit with you?"

The man smiles. "Yes, of course! I need help. Where is the old market?"

Ada sits in the red chair. Leo brings her small black coffee and her orange cake.

Ada looks at the map. "The old market? It is easy. You walk on this street. Then you turn left at the big tree."

They talk for thirty minutes. Ada draws on the map. The man writes in his notebook. They laugh.

At eight o'clock, the man stands up. "Thank you, Ada. Thank you, Leo. This is a very good café."

He goes out into the rain.

Ada drinks her coffee. "You know, Leo," she says, "I like the red chair."

Leo laughs. "It is your chair now."

Then a letter falls through the door. Leo picks it up. It is from the owner of the building.

Leo opens it. He reads it. He stops laughing.
