# The Wrong Screen

Baris ran through the airport with his bag on his back.

His plane was at gate fourteen. He looked at every sign. Twelve, thirteen... where was fourteen? He ran past a coffee shop and a row of chairs.

Finally he saw it. GATE 14 — FLIGHT 220 — CLOSED.

"No, no, no," he said.

A woman in a blue uniform stood by the door. "Sir, this flight is closed," she said. "The doors close ten minutes before the plane leaves. I am sorry."

"But my watch says I have five minutes!"

"Your watch is wrong, or the screen changed the time. It happens." She pointed at a big screen above her head. "Look. The plane is going now."

Baris looked through the window. The plane was already moving away from the airport building.

He sat down on the floor, right there by the closed door. His job interview was tomorrow morning, in another city, at nine o'clock. He needed this plane.

"What can I do?" he asked the woman.

"Go to the help desk. It is near gate one. They can find you another flight."

Baris picked up his bag and walked, slowly this time. There was no reason to run anymore.

At the help desk, a young man looked at his computer for a long time.

"The next flight to your city is tomorrow morning," he said. "At seven."

"Tomorrow morning? My interview is at nine!"

"Seven a.m., sir. You can still make it. But there are no flights tonight. This is the last one, and it is full anyway."

Baris put his head in his hands. "So I must wait here all night?"

"You can wait here, or find a hotel near the airport." The man typed something. "I am going to give you a free ticket for tomorrow's flight, because of the mistake with the screen. That is the best I can do."

Baris took the new ticket. Seven a.m. It was going to be close, but it was going to work.

He looked around the airport. It was late, almost eleven at night. Most of the shops were closed. Rows of chairs stood empty under bright lights.

He was going to spend the night here.

# A Stranger's Coat

Baris found a row of chairs near a window and sat down.

The airport was quiet now. A cleaner moved slowly across the floor with a big machine. Somewhere far away, a phone rang and rang.

He tried to sleep, but the chairs were hard and the lights were too bright. He thought about his interview. He thought about the job, the new city, the plan he made for months.

"You can't sleep either?"

Baris opened his eyes. An older woman sat two chairs away. She had a small bag and a warm brown coat.

"No," Baris said. "I missed my flight. Now I have an interview tomorrow, and I am not sure I can make it."

"I know that feeling," the woman said. "My flight is broken too — the plane has a problem with the engine. I am going to see my daughter. She is going to have a baby soon."

"I'm sorry," Baris said. "That must be hard, waiting here."

"It is hard, but I can wait. Babies do not follow a schedule, and I don't have to either." She smiled. "What is your interview for?"

"A teacher job. English, for young students." Baris looked down at his hands. "It is a good school. I really want this job."

"Then you are going to get it," the woman said simply. "You came all this way. You are here at midnight, still trying. That says something about you."

Baris almost laughed. "I hope the school thinks the same thing."

They talked for a while about her daughter, about his students, about the strange lights and noises of the airport at night. Around one in the morning, the woman took off her brown coat and gave it to him.

"Use this," she said. "It is cold in here, and you need to sleep, even a little."

"I can't take your coat."

"You can give it back in the morning. I am not using it right now." She closed her eyes. "Sleep, Baris. Seven o'clock comes fast."

Baris put the coat over his legs. It was warm and it smelled like coffee and old paper. He finally closed his eyes.

He woke up at half past five. The airport was starting to wake up too — more people, more lights, the smell of fresh bread from a shop nearby.

The woman was already awake, drinking tea from a paper cup.

"Good morning," she said. "Gate seven, seven o'clock. You have time."

Baris gave her back the coat. "Thank you. For this, and for last night. Without it, I don't think I was going to sleep at all."

"Go get your job," she said, and pushed him towards the gate.

He picked up his bag and walked fast, then ran a little, through gate seven, onto the plane. As it moved into the sky, he looked down at the airport, small and grey below the clouds.

He landed at eight thirty. He found a taxi. He ran into the school building at eight fifty-eight, out of breath, but on time.

The interview went well, and they gave him the job. Later, he walked out of the building and thought about the woman with the brown coat. He thought about the daughter she was going to meet.

He hoped her flight left on time too.
