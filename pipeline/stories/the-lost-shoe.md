# A Big Market Day

Aylin walked with her mother. The shop was busy. Big bags of oranges were in rows. Aylin looked at every table.

"Stay close," her mother said. "It is busy today."

Aylin wanted a red apple. She let go of her mother's hand. She ran to the fruit table.

She put her hand up for an apple. A box was near her leg. Her shoe was on the ground now.

She looked at her leg. Her shoe was gone.

People walked fast. Many legs went past her. She did not see it anywhere.

"Mom!" she called.

Her mother turned around. "What is wrong?"

"My shoe," Aylin said. "It is gone."

Her mother looked at Aylin's leg. One shoe, and no shoe on the other side.

"Where did it go?"

"I do not know. It is just gone."

Her mother looked under the fruit table. She looked behind the boxes. No shoe.

"The bus comes at twelve," her mother said. "We need to find it fast."

Aylin's leg felt cold with no shoe on it. She looked at the many legs near her. Too many people. Too much noise.

A man with a fruit cart walked past them.

"Sorry," Aylin's mother said to him. "Did you see a small shoe? It is white, with a blue flower."

The man said, "No, sorry."

Aylin's mother looked tired now. She looked at the clock on the wall. It was eleven thirty.

"Think, Aylin," her mother said. "Where were you standing?"

"Right here," Aylin said. "By the apples."

They looked near the table again. Boxes and bags and more bags. No white shoe.

Then Aylin saw a girl. The girl sat near the fish table. She was cleaning fish with her father.

The girl looked at Aylin's leg. Her face was happy.

"I think I saw your shoe," she said.

# The Girl by the Fish Stall

Aylin walked to the girl. "You saw my shoe?"

"Yes," the girl said. "A dog took it."

"A dog? Where?"

"That way," the girl said. "Past the bread table."

Aylin's mother did not look happy. "A dog has your shoe now."

"We can find it," the girl said. She got up. "My name is Defne. I know this shop well."

They walked fast past the bread table. The smell of bread was strong. Aylin did not stop. She looked only for a dog.

"There!" Defne said.

A small brown dog sat near a fish box. The white shoe was between its front legs. The blue flower was in the dog's mouth.

"Do not run at him," Defne said. "He will run away."

Aylin stopped. She walked with small steps.

"Hello," she said in a quiet voice. "That is my shoe."

The dog looked up. He did not run. He looked at her with big eyes.

Defne took a small piece of fish from her pocket. She put it on the ground, away from the shoe.

The dog left the shoe. He walked to the fish and ate it fast.

Aylin ran to the shoe. She picked it up. The flower had a small hole now, but the shoe was fine.

"Thank you," Aylin said to Defne.

"No problem," Defne said. "Dogs here like shoes. I do not know why."

Aylin's mother laughed. "Thank you very much," she said. "You saved our morning."

Aylin sat on a box and put her shoe on. It was good, hole and all.

"We need to go," her mother said. "The bus is soon."

Aylin got up. She looked at Defne. "Can I see you again?"

"I am here every Saturday," Defne said. "By the fish table."

"Then I will come another Saturday," Aylin said.

They ran to the bus. Aylin looked back one time. Defne had fish in one hand, and a big smile on her face.
